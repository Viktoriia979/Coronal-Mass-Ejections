Coronal Mass Ejections

Coronal Mass Ejections, or CMEs, are huge clouds of plasma and magnetic fields that are released from the Sun’s corona into space. They can contain billions of tonnes of material and travel through space at very high speeds. CMEs are much larger than solar flares and can travel towards Earth.

CMEs form when the Sun’s magnetic fields become twisted and unstable. When these magnetic fields suddenly rearrange or break apart, they can release a large amount of energy and push a huge cloud of plasma away from the Sun. CMEs often happen near sunspots and can occur at the same time as solar flares.

If a CME travels towards Earth, it can interact with Earth’s magnetic field and cause a powerful geomagnetic storm. These storms can interfere with satellites, GPS, radio communication and electricity networks. They can also create bright auroras, such as the Northern Lights. Although CMEs can cause problems for technology, Earth’s atmosphere and magnetic field protect people on the ground from most of their effects.
